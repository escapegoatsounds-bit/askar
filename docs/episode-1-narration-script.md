## PART 1 · LIFE AT THE LAKE (0:00–3:00)

**1 · 0:00 · River at dusk** · [Card: LAND OF REBIRTH · Episode 1 · Where the Nile Breathes Life]
[No voice: wind, water, a single cane flute]

**2 · 0:10 · Old Nebet**
Should we have a little talk? I wasn't born by this river. Nobody I loved was.

**3 · 0:20 · Into the desert** · [Card: I · THE LAKE — Nabta Playa, Western Desert · c. 4500 BC · the last herders of the Green Sahara]
[No voice: music and ambience]

**4 · 0:30 · The lake of Nabta**
I was born beside a lake… in the middle of the desert. Yes. A lake. Out there.

**5 · 0:40 · The summer rains**
Every summer the monsoon rain came, and the lake woke up. The grass came back. The gazelle came back. So did we.

**6 · 0:50 · The herd**
Our cattle were our wealth, and our pride. We almost never ate them. Only a fool eats his own future.

**7 · 1:00 · Milk and blood**
We lived on their milk… and a little of their blood, drawn from the neck. It sounds cruel. It kept us alive.

**8 · 1:10 · Wild grain**
We gathered wild sorghum and millet. Nobody planted them. The rain did the planting for us.

**9 · 1:20 · Grinding**
Every morning the women ground the seed between two stones. You could hear the camp waking up by that sound.

**10 · 1:30 · The walk-in well**
When the dry months came, we dug walk-in wells into the lake bed, and climbed down after the water.

**11 · 1:40 · What we wore**
We wore the skins of our animals, shells traded from far away, and beads cut from ostrich eggshell.

**12 · 1:50 · The palette**
And we painted our eyes with green stone, ground on a palette. To be beautiful. To keep off the glare and the flies.

**13 · 2:00 · Night music**
At night we danced, and the rattles talked to the stars.

**14 · 2:10 · The stone circle**
Our elders read the sun and the seasons in a circle of standing stones. They knew when the rain was due.

**15 · 2:20 · Cattle honoured**
When a great cow died, we buried her like a person, under a mound of stones.

**16 · 2:30 · A funeral**
We buried our dead curled as if asleep, with the most beautiful things we owned: polished pots, palettes, shells.

**17 · 2:40 · The gazelle-horn man**
So they would not arrive in the next life… with empty hands.

**18 · 2:50 · The water birds**
Every spring the water birds came. My father said they knew the way to a great river in the east. Remember that.

## PART 2 · THE DROUGHT (3:00–5:00)

**19 · 3:00 · Clouds, no rain** · [Card: II · THE DROUGHT — the summer rains retreat south and the Western Desert dries]
[No voice: dry thunder]

**20 · 3:10 · The first dry year**
Then one summer, the rain forgot us. Each year it stopped a little farther south. The first year… we shrugged.

**21 · 3:20 · No birds**
The second year, the birds did not come. That frightened the old ones more than the thirst.

**22 · 3:30 · Thin cattle**
You could count the cattle's ribs from across the camp. You could count ours too.

**23 · 3:40 · Nothing to grind**
[No voice: music and ambience]

**24 · 3:50 · Beads for water**
I traded my bracelet for water. Then my beads. Then I stopped counting what I traded.

**25 · 4:00 · A poor funeral**
That year we buried a child with nothing. We had nothing left to give.

**26 · 4:10 · A gift to the sky**
So we gave the sky our best cow. The sky took her. It gave nothing back.

**27 · 4:20 · The elders' fire**
The elders argued until the fire died. Stay with our dead… or walk east, to a river none of us had ever seen?

**28 · 4:30 · The water skin**
[No voice: music and ambience]

**29 · 4:40 · Leaving the lake**
At dawn my father tied his water skin. That was the answer. We walked toward the sunrise, to the river.

**30 · 4:50 · The lions**
On the ninth night we found water. So had the lions. They were starving too.

## PART 4 · THE RIVER PEOPLE (8:00–11:00)

**49 · 8:00 · The little hands** · [Card: IV · THE RIVER PEOPLE — the Badarians, first farmers of Upper Egypt · c. 4400 BC]
Someone had been here long before us. They left their hands on the wall… or something like hands.

**50 · 8:10 · Our hands**
So I left ours beside them. Mine, and my son's. So the rock would remember us too.

**51 · 8:20 · The storm's gift**
The storm that tried to bury us… left water in the rocks.

**52 · 8:30 · The water birds**
Then the birds rose and flew east. Water birds do not fly toward nothing.

**53 · 8:40 · His cry**
I walked until I couldn't. And when I fell, my son cried. And someone heard.

**54 · 8:50 · Milk**
Herders, walking east like us. A woman I had never seen gave me milk. I have never tasted anything better.

**55 · 9:00 · The green line**
Then I saw it.

**56 · 9:10 · Hands in the river**
I put my hands in the water. It was cold… and it did not end.

**57 · 9:20 · The river people**
The river people took us in. They didn't ask where we came from. The desert was written all over us.

**58 · 9:30 · Sown fields**
Here, people planted their seed: emmer and barley. The river people said it came from the north, long ago.

**59 · 9:40 · Planting**
They didn't beg the sky. The river rose every year by itself, and left black mud behind.

**60 · 9:50 · The sickle**
They cut the grain with sickles of wood, set with teeth of flint, sharp as a lion's tooth.

**61 · 10:00 · Linen**
They wore cloth made from a plant, called linen. The first time I touched it, I laughed. It was softer than any skin.

**62 · 10:10 · Rippled pots**
Their pots were thinner than my fingernail, combed with ripples, like wind on water.

**63 · 10:20 · Fishing**
And the river fed everyone who reached it. Fish, fowl, grain. Every day. Without being asked.

**64 · 10:30 · River treasures**
They buried their dead facing the setting sun, like us. With ivory combs, green stone… and beads of a strange red metal.

**65 · 10:40 · Years later**
The desert took our people. The river gave us new ones.

**66 · 10:50 · Rebirth** · [Card: LAND OF REBIRTH]
Our hands are still on that wall. Let them stay there. We live here now.
