# Episode 1 · Where the Nile Breathes Life · Final narration script

*c. 4500 BC · Narrator: **Nebet**, speaking to herself and to the audience, like someone reading their own diary aloud. English (for dubbing).*
*Pace: about 2.2 words per second; every line fits inside its 10-second frame with room to breathe. [Brackets] are directions, not spoken.*

---

## PART 1 · LIFE AT THE LAKE (0:00–3:00)

**1 · 0:00** · [River at dusk. Wind, water, birds. Title card. No voice.]

**2 · 0:10** · [Old Nebet on the riverbank. Quiet, close to the mic.]
Should we have a little talk?
I wasn't born by this river. Nobody I loved was.

**3 · 0:20** · [The camera rises into the desert. No voice.]

**4 · 0:30**
I was born beside a lake… in the middle of the desert.
Yes. A lake. Out there.

**5 · 0:40**
Every summer the rain came, and the lake woke up.
The grass came back. The gazelle came back. So did we.

**6 · 0:50**
Our cattle were our wealth, and our pride.
We almost never ate them. Only a fool eats his own future.

**7 · 1:00**
We lived on their milk… and a little of their blood.
It sounds cruel. It kept us alive.

**8 · 1:10** · [Filler. Wild grain. No voice.]

**9 · 1:20**
Every morning the women ground wild seed between two stones.
You could hear the camp waking up by that sound.

**10 · 1:30**
When the dry months came, we dug down into the lake bed
and climbed in after the water.

**11 · 1:40** · [Nebet's portrait, orbit.]
We wore the skins of our animals, shells from far away,
and beads cut from ostrich eggs.

**12 · 1:50** · [Malachite palette.]
And we painted our eyes with green stone. To be beautiful. To be protected.

**13 · 2:00** · [Night dance. Rattles. Let the music lead, voice late in the frame.]
At night we danced, and the rattles talked to the stars.

**14 · 2:10**
Our elders read the sun and the seasons in a circle of standing stones.
They always knew when the rain was coming.

**15 · 2:20**
When a great cow died, we buried her like a person.
That is how much they meant to us.

**16 · 2:30**
And we buried our people with the most beautiful things we owned:
polished pots, palettes, shells.

**17 · 2:40** · [Gazelle-horn elder. Soft.]
So they would not arrive in the next life… with empty hands.

**18 · 2:50** · [Water birds. Wedja held up to the sky. Warm, a smile in the voice.]
And every spring, the water birds came.
My father said they knew the way to a great river in the east.
Remember that.

---

## PART 2 · THE RAIN FORGETS US (3:00–5:00)

**19 · 3:00** · [Dry thunder. No voice.]

**20 · 3:10**
Then one summer, the rain forgot us.
The first year… we shrugged.

**21 · 3:20**
The second year, the birds did not come.
That frightened the old ones more than the thirst.

**22 · 3:30**
You could count the cattle's ribs from across the camp.
You could count ours too.

**23 · 3:40** · [Empty grinding stone. No voice. Let the silence hurt.]

**24 · 3:50**
I traded my bracelet for water.
Then my beads. Then I stopped counting what I traded.

**25 · 4:00** · [A child's grave without pots. Slow.]
That year, we buried a child with nothing.
We had nothing left to give.

**26 · 4:10**
So we gave the sky our best cow.
The sky took her. It gave nothing back.

**27 · 4:20**
The elders argued until the fire died.
Stay with our dead… or walk east, to a river none of us had ever seen?

**28 · 4:30** · [Her father ties his water skin. No voice.]

**29 · 4:40**
At dawn my father tied his water skin. That was the answer.
We walked toward the sunrise.

**30 · 4:50** · [The lions. Short, flat, numb.]
On the ninth night we found water. So had the lions.
They were starving too.

---

## PART 3 · THE STORM PRAYER (5:00–8:00)

*[The song carries this part. Only three lines are spoken.]*

**31 · 5:00** · [Song begins: hushing wind. No voice.]

**32 · 5:10** · [Whispered, almost to herself.]
Then the horizon began to breathe.

**33–38 · 5:20–6:10** · [Verse 1 and Chorus 1. No voice. The wall, the run, the shield, the water skin torn away.]

**39 · 6:20** · [Between the call and response of Verse 2. Raw, close.]
I didn't know their names. I called them anyway.

**40–44 · 6:30–7:10** · [The eye of the storm, the rise, the giants, Set, THE DROP. No voice.]

**45 · 7:20** · [Over the drop chorus, slow and certain, almost a prayer answered.]
And the last one rose from water.
Water… in the middle of the sand.

**46–48 · 7:30–7:50** · [Choir outro, the rock, the storm passing, her breath. No voice.]

---

## PART 4 · THE RIVER PEOPLE (8:00–11:00)

**49 · 8:00** · [Inside the cave. Wonder.]
Someone had been here before us. Long before.
They left their hands on the wall… or something like hands.

**50 · 8:10** · [Blowing ochre. Tender.]
So I left ours beside them. Mine, and yours, my son.
So the rock would remember us too.

**51 · 8:20**
The storm that tried to bury us… left water in the rocks.

**52 · 8:30** · [The flute theme returns.]
And then the birds rose, and flew east.
Water birds do not fly toward nothing.

**53 · 8:40**
I walked until I couldn't. And when I fell, my son cried.
And someone heard.

**54 · 8:50**
Herders, walking east like us. A woman I had never seen gave me milk.
I have never tasted anything better.

**55 · 9:00** · [The green line. Let the music swell first; voice at the end of the frame.]
Then I saw it.

**56 · 9:10** · [Hands in the river. A breath before speaking.]
I put my hands in the water.
It was cold… and it did not end.

**57 · 9:20**
The river people took us in.
They didn't ask where we came from. The desert was written all over us.

**58 · 9:30** · [Fields. No voice.]

**59 · 9:40**
Here, they didn't beg the sky.
The river came every year, by itself, and left black mud behind. And in the mud, they planted seed.

**60 · 9:50** · [Flint sickle. No voice.]

**61 · 10:00**
They wore cloth made from a plant.
The first time I touched it, I laughed. It was softer than any skin.

**62 · 10:10** · [Rippled pots. No voice.]

**63 · 10:20**
And the river fed everyone who reached it.
Fish, fowl, grain. Every day. Without being asked.

**64 · 10:30**
They buried their dead facing the setting sun, like us.
With combs of ivory, green stone… and beads of a strange red metal.

**65 · 10:40** · [Old Nebet watching grown Wedja and his children. Warm, settled.]
The desert took our people.
The river gave us new ones.

**66 · 10:50** · [Final shot. Slow, last line, then music to card.]
Our hands are still on that wall. Let them stay there.
We live here now.

*[Card: LAND OF REBIRTH]*

---

**Word count:** about 780 spoken words in 11:00, leaving space for music, ambience and silence.
**Accuracy notes:** "red metal" = Badarian copper beads (among the earliest metal in Egypt). "A child buried with nothing" and the gazelle-horn elder follow real Gebel Ramlah burials. Nebet never names the gods; the song does.
